What we are working on

Individual studies, grouped under the lab's four research programmes. Each entry links to the publications and preprints it has produced so far.

Programme

Neuropolitics of Identity

What underpins a sense of social belonging and political obligation, and what shifts it.

Mapping the moral landscape of Europe Active

Constantine Kyritsopoulos · Adam Moore · Laura Cram

Moral foundations — the core values that shape political ideology and communication — vary across Europe, and earlier work found systematic differences between European and nationalist identities. This project runs a large-scale survey across Germany, Italy, Denmark and Poland to map those moral landscapes directly.

It is the foundation for the framing experiments below, and the published Scottish case study gives an early read on how moral and ideological divides track territorial identification.

Scotland paper OSF

Territorial identity triggers and behaviour Ongoing

Laura Cram · Adam Moore

A long-running experimental strand asking whether implicit identity cues change what people actually do, not just what they report. Ultimatum-game work showed that territorial identity triggers shift bargaining behaviour; the imaging arm asks what happens in the brain when national identity primes are present during empathy and social-exclusion tasks.

Political Psychology

Trust, political identity and the brain Ongoing

Adam Moore · Su-Jin Hong · Laura Cram · with Edinburgh Imaging

An interdisciplinary fMRI study of how political identity conditions whether information is trusted. Behavioural results and analysis code are openly available, and the design continues to inform the lab's political-communication work.

Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B

Neuropolitics of Political Communication

Why some messages read as authoritative or authentic, and what that means for the information environment.

Narratives at scale: LLMs for political narrative analysis Active

Luke Stephens · Clare Llewellyn · Laura Cram · with the wider lab

Narratives are central to human sense-making, and political actors use them strategically to shape interpretation and mobilise support. Narrative research has traditionally been qualitative and small-N, because it needs contextual understanding. This project asks whether large language models can reliably recover both the structure and the themes of narratives at scale, combining computational analysis with traditional qualitative methods.

CHR 2026

Auditing ideology in commercial LLMs Under review

Constantine Kyritsopoulos · Adam Moore · Laura Cram · Jake Van Clief · Clare Llewellyn

If large language models are becoming infrastructure for the digital public sphere, their own ideological tendencies matter. This audit measures authoritarianism in commercial models, treating LLMs as objects of psychometric study rather than only as instruments.

SSRN preprint

The Brexit Twitter archive Ongoing

Clare Llewellyn · Laura Cram · Robin Hill · Adrian Favero

One of the largest available corpora of Brexit-related tweets, built with explicit attention to how collection strategy biases the resulting dataset. It has supported published work on shifting troll behaviour through the referendum debate, on Russian troll activity in the archive, and on how opinion moved within Twitter around the vote.

Communicating Europe Ongoing

Roberta Rocca · Katharina Lawall · Manos Tsakiris · Laura Cram

A computational analysis of how the European Commission's communication on Twitter has evolved — what the institution says, how that has changed, and what it implies for the relationship between supranational institutions and their publics.

J. Comput. Soc. Sci.

Neuropolitics of Public Policy

How policy environments, physiology and inter-group dynamics shape decisions and their acceptance.

Moral framing and asylum policy Active

Laura Cram · Adam Moore · Constantine Kyritsopoulos

Building on the moral-landscape survey, this experimental work asks whether moral framing can shift public support for EU solidarity policies — particularly among groups usually resistant to them. The aim is to identify moral rhetoric that resonates across diverse European populations rather than only with the already-persuaded.

OSF

Norms, prejudice and social change Active

Feiteng Long

How norm messages can reduce prejudice, promote collective action and mitigate polarisation, particularly regarding migrants and LGBT+ groups. The project combines field experiments in real groups with psychophysiological measurement — cardiovascular and skin conductance responses that distinguish whether people experience social change as threat or as challenge.

Political Psychology Psychophysiology

Early life adversity and immigration attitudes In preparation

Arman Prangere · Our Mind Summer Project Scholarship

An examination of the potential relationship between early life adversity and restrictive attitudes toward immigration, exploring psychological pathways such as threat sensitivity, anxiety and social trust. The work aims to deepen understanding of how personal histories influence political attitudes, and how those insights might support more constructive public debate.

Neuropolitics of Political Behaviour

What drives individual and collective political action, measured across brains, bodies and timelines.

Moral foundations and political attitudes Ongoing

Adam Moore and colleagues

Moral trade-off tasks are used to recover the foundational representations underlying political attitudes, testing whether they predict variance that standard measures miss. A related strand models political polarisation and fractionalisation as the outcome of rational, values-based inference in agent-based graph networks.

Br. J. Soc. Psychol. Preprint

Power motives, dominance and political preference Ongoing

Adam Moore · Felix Suessenbach · Andrew Ithurburn

A programme on social power motives — dominance, prestige and leadership — and what they predict: risk preferences, utilitarian choices in moral dilemmas, conspiracy beliefs, and candidate support.

Eur. J. Personality Motivation Science

Behavioural indicators in human–agent interaction Ongoing

Sara Dalzel-Job · Robin Hill

Using eye-tracking, electrodermal activity, heart rate and facial expression to identify when an interaction with an artificial agent is going wrong, and how the way information is presented affects situational readiness — including for frontline emergency workers.
